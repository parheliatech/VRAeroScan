## Full description (4000)

VRAeroScan labels what is overhead. Put on a pair of Viture XR glasses, plug them into your
phone, and the planes, the ISS, Starlink trains and about 16,000 other satellites appear
exactly where they really are in the sky, with names, altitude and pass times.

• Live aircraft from public ADS-B data: callsign, type, altitude, with a different silhouette
  for airliners, private planes, helicopters and military aircraft
• Satellites from CelesTrak orbit data, computed on the phone: crewed stations, Starlink,
  low, medium and geostationary orbits. Satellites are drawn even when you could not see
  them by eye, so you can find where they are.
• Identify: aim a circle at anything and get a card with its details
• Set north in seconds: face north, sight the pole star, or nudge the sky with your finger
• View the sky from the ground, from the centre of the Earth, or from as far out as
  geostationary orbit
• The phone becomes the controller: choose which groups to show, label sizes and more

What you need: Viture XR glasses (Pro XR tested), an Android phone that outputs video over
USB-C, GPS and an internet connection. Without the glasses the app opens on the phone screen
for a look around.

Free, with a banner ad on the phone's control panel. Nothing is ever shown on the glasses.
Open source (MIT): github.com/parheliatech/VRAeroScan
Published by Parhelia Technology, LLC. Support: info@parheliatech.com

Data: aircraft positions from adsb.lol, orbits from CelesTrak. Not affiliated with Viture.
Never look at the Sun through glasses or optics.
